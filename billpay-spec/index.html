Compliance & kiosk spec · BillPay-POS

a clinic's web app, rebuilt as an iPad and a kiosk, without breaking PCI or HIPAA

The clinics were already running this as a web app at the front desk. The ask was to move it to iPad, and add a kiosk mode next to it so a patient could pay without a staff member touching their card. There was no design file and no handoff, just the existing web app to study and a solo iOS developer to work out PCI and HIPAA compliance well enough to pass clinic standards. Below are the real requirements behind the kiosk's patient-facing security, the payment flow, the audit log, and multi-tenant isolation, written as user stories with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, the same way I handed them to myself before writing a line of code.

Clinic names, exact patient-data fields, and vendor contract terms are left out for confidentiality. The requirements and acceptance criteria themselves are real.

Kiosk mode

a patient paying alone is a different product than a staff member taking payment

Once a patient could check out with no staff member present, every failure mode I'd already handled for the front desk needed a second, stricter version for someone with no training and no one to ask.

Nothing left behind

As a patient, I want the screen to show nothing about my payment the moment I walk away

  • Given a transaction completes or is abandoned, when the patient steps away or the idle timer fires, then the screen resets to idle, no receipt or card detail left on display
  • Given the kiosk returns to idle, when the next patient approaches, then nothing about the previous patient's name, amount, or session is visible or recoverable on screen
Not even staff

As a patient, I want my payment details inaccessible to clinic staff and to every other patient at the kiosk

  • Given a kiosk transaction completes, when staff view the front-desk system, then they see that payment was made, never the card or transaction detail behind it
  • Given a patient walks away mid-payment, when the next patient starts a session, then the previous session is fully torn down first, never paused or resumable by anyone else
Failure modes I designed around before writing any code
A patient walks away mid-payment, on purpose or by accident
A receipt or confirmation lingers on screen for the next patient to see
The kiosk needs its own idle-reset timing, separate from the 60-minute staff session rule, since no one's watching it expire
A patient can't read or act on an error state without a staff member nearby to help
Payments

two surfaces, one consistent record

A terminal and a Bluetooth reader at the desk, the same reader flow at the kiosk, and manual entry as the fallback everywhere, because clinics don't run identical hardware and not every patient wants a staff member involved.

Payment routing

As a front-desk user, I want to take payment however the clinic is equipped that day, terminal, reader, or manual

  • Given a Bluetooth reader is paired, when a payment starts, then the reader and the terminal are both offered, and manual entry stays available as a fallback
  • Given a manual entry, when the front desk keys in card details, then the same audit entry is written as for a terminal or reader payment
Offline handling

As a patient paying by card, I want a dropped connection mid-swipe to never double-charge or lose my payment

  • Given the network drops while a card payment is in flight, when the app detects the drop, then the transaction is cached locally, not silently retried
  • Given a cached transaction exists, when the connection comes back, then it reconciles automatically, and the user sees the result, not a retry prompt
Where the spec came from

There was no internal team for the AnyPay reader integration, so making it work meant getting on calls directly with AnywhereCommerce's own engineering team until the integration was clear enough to build against. None of it was documented anywhere the old web system had touched.

Audit log

six actions, written down, never erased

PCI and HIPAA both hold a payment log to the same bar: the audit requirement doesn't do anything on its own, it only works if it's structurally impossible to edit or delete.

Append-only

As a compliance reviewer, I want every touch of patient payment data logged, with no way for anyone to remove the record

  • Given any of the six defined actions on patient payment data occurs, when it happens, then it's written to an append-only log automatically, not on request
  • Given a write to an existing log entry is attempted, when it reaches the data layer, then it's rejected regardless of the requesting user's role
Session vs. payment

As a compliance officer, I want sessions to expire on schedule without breaking a payment already in progress

  • Given a session hits its 60-minute regulatory expiry, when a card payment is currently in flight, then the payment finishes and the session expires immediately after
  • Given no payment is in flight, when the 60 minutes elapse, then the session ends immediately, no grace period
Tenancy

one platform, clinics that can't touch each other's money

Multi-tenant credential isolation, designed with the backend team so a bug in one clinic's flow can't reach into another's.

As a clinic administrator

I want my clinic's funds and data fully isolated from every other clinic on the platform, so a bug or a bad actor elsewhere can't touch either.

  • Given two clinics share the platform, when either processes a payment, then no credential or credential-adjacent action can reference the other clinic's tenant ID
As a support engineer

I want to debug one clinic's issue without being able to see or touch data from any other clinic.

  • Given a support case for one clinic, when logs are accessed, then access is scoped to that clinic's tenant only

This is the same shape I hand every spec in, staff-facing or patient-facing: user story first, acceptance criteria second, nothing left for a developer, or a patient standing alone at a kiosk, to guess at.
